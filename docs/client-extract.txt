Directors' Report For the Year Ended 31 December 2025
ADIKE MIROFINANCE BANK LIMITED was incorporated in Nigeria under the Companies and Allied Matters Act as a private limilted 
liability company on 28th November, 2023. The Bank was granted a licence by the Central Bank of Nigeria to operate as a Microfinance 
Bank in June, 2024. It commenced banking business on January 21, 2025. The Bank has authorized share capital of One hundred and 
fifty million (150 million) shares.
Gross earning of the Bank stood at N38.2 million during the year and the loss before tax of N44 million. The summary of the financial 
performance of the Bank is as follows:
The Bank' principal activitity is provision of microfinance banking services to individual and micro business customers. The services 
include collection of customers' deposit, granting of loans to micro businesses and individuals and related services.

Employment of Physically Challenged Persons
The Bank policy supports employment of physically challenged persons in consideration of their abilities and work deliverables. 
Descrimination of physically challenged persons in the Bank's employment and carrier progression is prohibited. Where any member of 
staff becomes disabled, it is the policy of the Bank that necessary steps should be taken to ensure that their employment continues and 
arrangement made for appropriate training to enable the staff remain relevant at work.

It is the policy of the Bank to always maintain a fair employment policy that allows equal opportunities to all persons in its employment; 
and maintain a remuneration package capable of attracting best workforce to drive the Bank's mandate of providing improved quality 
and exciting banking services to its customers.
Health, Safety and Welfare of Staff
The health and safety of the staff are of atmost importance to the Bank. Accordingly, the Bank enforces its health and safety rules and 
practices at the work environment. The remuneration policy of the Bank provides for medical allowance to all staff in various cadre. The 
Bank also has group life insurance policy to provide for families of deceased staff who died in active service. Fire-fighting equipments 
are at all strategic positions in the Bank premises and fire drills are conducted regularly to sensitize staff on fire prevention measures.

Corporate Governance Report  For the Year Ended 31 December 2025 (Contd)
We are committed to ensuring ethical bahaviours, high standards, transparency, openness, integrity of our staff and global best practices in the Bank. To 
achieve these, the Bank has designed a mechanism that would enable both internal and external persons report activities that are short of set standards. The 
whistle-blowing channels allow whistle blowers to report breaches of the Bank internal policies, processes, procedures and unethical activities. We have 
designated communication lines through which breaches can be reported for proper action, and the reporting channels protect the identity of the whietle-
blower.
The Bank has a remuneration policy of paying sitting allowance to non executive directors while the executive director is on monthly salary. The Bank also 
has retirement package for directors who has served out their term of office meritoriously.
Corporate Social Responsibility
The Bank recognizes corporate social responsility initiatives as a means of giving back to the society. The Bank has, in the past, reached out to the 
Community and its enviros in some of its programmes aimed at impacting the public. No CSR programme was undertaken during the reporting year.
Sustainability and Environmental Issues
Impact of the Bank's activities in the environment is a consideration in the policy making process. We recognize that environment management is 
fundamental in sustaining our operations. The Bank is committed to working with key stakeholders in ensuring a conducive atmosphere for business. As 
part of our contribution to reducing our environmental footprint, the Bank is stategically planning to invest greatly in online banking to reduce significantly 
paper work and embrace digital banking. The Bank believes that sustainbility of its business depends materially on how it interact with the environment.
The Bank is also investing in solar energy to reduce carbon emission in its environment. 
Employment and Labour Relation
The Bank is committed to promoting elimination of all forms of discrimination in employment. At ADIKE MFB, staff are allowed to contribute to key 
decisions as it affect them. The Bank, through the Finance and General Purpose Committee of the Board, interfaces with staff on issues relating to their 
welfare and other employment related issues. We believe that staff is the greatest asset of the Bank, hence, staff matters are taken seriously to ensure that 
the Bank provides exciting services to its customers.
Human Right
Our Bank has deep respect for human right and related matters. We have labour policy guides that is similar in content to the Universal Declaration of 
Human Right. The rights of all people are recognized irrespective of persons, age, gender, faith, background or even social status.  People living with 
disability, HIV and related issues are not discriminated against in ADIKE MFB. The Bank has continued to maintain whistle-blowing channels through 
which human right abuses can be reported for appropriate action. We take human right issues seriously such that any customer with records of human 
right abuses is denied access to our credit facilities. Officers of the Bank proven to have human right abuse records are sanctioned according to the policy of 
the Bank.

Bribery and Corruption Policy
The Bank frowns at behaviours that are unbecoming and unethical. Bribery and corruption are serious misconduct and appropriate sanctions for such 
offences are well-documented in the Bank' policy. Our whistle-blowing mechanism provides opportunities for both internal and external persons to reports 
cases of bribery and corruption. No case of bribery and corruption was reported during the reporting year.

Interest income is recognised on a time-proportionate basis using the effective interest method. It includes 
interest income from cash and cash equivalents.
The effective interest method is a method of calculating the amortised cost of a financial asset or a financial
liability and of allocating the interest income or interest expense over the relevant period. The effective
interest rate is the rate that exactly discounts estimated future cash payments or receipts through the expected
life of the financial instrument or, when appropriate, a shorter period to the net carrying amount of the
financial asset or financial liability. When calculating the effective interest rate, the Company estimates cash
flows considering all contractual terms of the financial instrument (for example, prepayment options) but
does not consider future credit losses. The calculation includes all fees and points paid or received between
parties to the contract that are an integral part of the effective interest rate, transaction costs and all other
premiums or discounts.
Once a financial asset or a group of similar financial assets has been written down as a result of an impairment
loss, interest income is recognised using the rate of interest used to discount the future cash flows for the
purpose of measuring the impairment loss.

Determination of fair value
For financial instruments traded in active markets, the determination of fair values of financial assets and
financial liabilities is based on quoted market prices or dealer price quotations. 
A financial instrument is regarded as quoted in an active market if quoted prices are readily and regularly
available from an exchange, dealer, broker, industry Company, pricing service or regulatory agency, and those
prices represent actual and regularly occurring market transactions on an arm’s length basis. If the above
criteria are not met, the market is regarded as being inactive. Indications that a market is inactive are when
there is a wide bid-offer spread or significant increase in the bid-offer spread or there are few recent
transactions.
For all other financial instruments, fair value is determined using valuation techniques. In these techniques,
fair values are estimated from observable data in respect of similar financial instruments, using models to
estimate the present value of expected future cash flows or other valuation techniques, using inputs (for
example, NIBOR yield curve, FX rates, volatilities and counterparty spreads) existing at the dates of the
statement of financial position.
The output of a model is always an estimate or approximation of a value that cannot be determined with
certainty, and valuation techniques employed may not fully reflect all factors relevant to the positions the
Company holds. Valuations are therefore adjusted, where appropriate, to allow for additional factors including
model risks, liquidity risk and counterparty credit risk. Based on the established fair value model governance
policies, and related controls and procedures applied, management believes that these valuation adjustments
are necessary and appropriate to fairly state the values of financial instruments carried at fair value in the
statement of financial position. Price data and parameters used in the measurement procedures applied are
generally reviewed carefully and adjusted, if necessary – particularly in view of the current market
developments.

ECL for 12-months is determined by the product of probability of default and loss given default and exposure

Definition of Parameters (Measurement Variables)
Probability of Default- an estimate of likelihood of default over a given time horizon, calculated based on 
credit rating models of various market segments of the Bank. The credit model (rating) is internally compiled 
which include qualitative and quantitative factors. The Bank assign weight to PDs, taking into account default 
portfolio of previous years, and more weight is usually assign to current period for the sake of prudence. 
Importantly, PDs of financial instruments under lifetime ECL bucket will be adjusted by macroeconomic 
variables (inflation rate forecast or GDP), to incorporate forward-looking information.
Loss Given Default- an estimate of amount loss in event of default. The Bank estimates LGD based on recovery 
rate in the past. Where there are collaterals, the present value of such collaterals will be determined and hair-
cut adjustment (according to CBN's Circular) done. The resultant amount will be compared with outstanding 
balance and where the PV of the collateral after hair-cut adjustment is less than oustanding loan balance, the 
difference is the loss at default. However, LGD is calliberated for different economic scenarios to incorporate 
changes in macroeconomic variables. This is particularly for financial instruments under lifetime ECL.
Exposure at Default- expected exposure in event of default. The Bank EAD represents outstanding loan 
balances in various stages. Where the Bank has granted revolving credit, the EAD would include the amount 
drawn, as well as, future amounts that may be drawn under the loan agreements.
Incorporation of Forward-looking Information
The Bank incorporates forward-looking information in assessing whether the credit risk of a financial 
instrument, after initial recognition, has increased significantly; and measurement of ECL allowance. Based 
on advice from financial experts and external data, the Bank develop a base case view of future direction of 
applicable economic variables. This will involve development of two or more scenarios and considering 
relative outcome of each scenario. External information is obtained from goverment bodies and monetary 
agencies in Nigeria.
Practically, the base case view represents the most likely outcome, while other scenarios represent more 
optimistic and more pessimistic outcomes. It is the practice of the Bank to carry-out stress testing of extreme 
shocks to calliberate its determination of other scenarios. As a way of being proactive in credit risk 
management, the Bank has documented key drivers of credit risk and credit losses for its financial 
instruments by analyzing historical data. This way, the relationship between macroeconomic variables and 
deliquencies of financial obligation of customers is known.

Income tax
(a) Current income tax
Income tax payable/(receivable) is calculated on the basis of the applicable tax law in the respective
jurisdiction and is recognised as an expense/(income) for the period except to the extent that current tax
related to items that are charged or credited in other comprehensive income or directly to equity. In these
circumstances, current tax is charged or credited to other comprehensive income or to equity (for example,
current tax on of available-for-sale investment).
Where tax losses can be relieved only by carry-forward against taxable profits of future periods, a deductible
temporary difference arises. Those losses carried forward are set off against deferred tax liabilities carried in
the statement of financial position.
The Company does not offset income tax liabilities and current income tax assets.
(b) Deferred income tax
Deferred income tax is provided in full, using the liability method, on temporary differences arising between
the tax bases of assets and liabilities and their carrying amounts in the financial statements. Deferred income
tax is determined using tax rates (and laws) that have been enacted or substantially enacted by the date of the
statement of financial position and are expected to apply when the related deferred income tax asset is realised 
or the deferred income tax liability is settled.
The principal temporary differences arise from depreciation of property, plant and equipment, revaluation of
certain financial assets and liabilities and provisions for pensions and other post-retirement benefits.
However, the deferred income tax is not accounted for if it arises from initial recognition of an asset or liability
in a transaction.
The tax effects of carry-forwards of unused losses, unused tax credits and other deferred tax assets are
recognised when it is probable that future taxable profit will be available against which these losses and other
temporary differences can be utilised.
The amount of deferred tax provided is based on the expected manner of realisation or settlement of the 
carrying amount of the asset or liability and is not discounted. Deferred tax assets are reviewed at each 
balance sheet date and are reduced to the extent that it is no longer probable that the related tax benefit will 
be realised.
Borrowings are recognized initially at fair value net of transaction cost incurred. Borrowings are subsequently
measured at amortized cost. Any difference between the proceeds (net of transaction cost) and redemption
value is recognised in the income statement over the period of the borrowing using the effective interest rate
method.
Fees paid on the establishment of loan facilities are recognised as transaction costs of the loan to the extent
that it is probable that some or all of the facility will be drawn down. In this case, the fee is deferred until the
draw-down occurs. To the extent there is no evidence that it is probable that some or all of the facility will be
drawn down, the fee is capitalised as a pre-payment for liquidity services and amortised over the period of the
facility to which it relates.

Provisions
Provisions are liabilities that are uncertain in amount and timing. Provision are recognised when the Company 
has a present legal or constructive obligation as a result of past events and it is more likely than not that an
outflow of resources will be required to settle the obligation and the amount can be reliably estimated.
Where there is a number of similar obligations, the likelihood that an outflow will be required in settlement is
determined by considering the class of obligations as a whole. A provision is recognised even if the likelihood
of an outflow with respect to any one item included in the same class of obligations may be small.
Provisions are measured at the present value of the expenditures expected to be required to settle the
obligation using a pre-tax rate that reflects current market assessments of the time value of money and the
risks specific to the obligation. 
Employee benefits
The company has a defined contribution plan.
A defined contribution plan is a pension plan under which the Company pays fixed contributions into a
separate entity. The Company has no legal or constructive obligations to pay further contributions if the fund
does not hold sufficient assets to pay all employees the benefits relating to employee service in the current and
prior periods.
In line with the Pension Reform Act 2014, the Company operates a defined contribution scheme; employees
are entitled to join the scheme on confirmation of their employment. The employee contributes 8% and the
Company contributes 10% of the employee's consolidated emolument respectively. The Company has no
further payment obligations once the contributions have been paid. The employee contributions are funded
through payroll deductions while the Company's contributions are recognised as employee benefit expenses
when they are due. Prepaid contributions are recognised as an asset to the extent that a cash refund or a
reduction in the future payments is available while unpaid contributions are recognized a  liability.
Contingent liabilities and contingent assets
A contingent liability is a possible obligation that arises from past event and whose existence will be confirmed
only by the occurrence or non-occurrence of one or more uncertain future events not wholly within the control
of the Company. It is not recognised because it is not likely that an outflow of resources will be required to
settle the obligation or the amount can not be reliably estimated.
Contingent liabilities normally comprise of legal claims under arbitration or court process in respect of which
a liability is not likely to occur.

Financial risk management
The company’s activities expose it to a variety of financial risks: market risk (including currency risk, fair value interest rate risk, cash flow interest rate risk
and price risk), credit risk and liquidity risk. The company’s overall risk management programme seeks to minimise potential adverse effects on it's
financial performance. 
Risk management is carried out by the risk department under policies approved by the board of directors. The risk department identifies and evaluates
financial risks in close co-operation with all operating units. The board provides written principles for overall risk management, as well as written policies
covering specific areas, such as interest rate risk, credit risk, use non-derivative financial instruments, and investment of excess liquidity.
Credit risk is the risk of suffering financial loss, should any of the company’s clients or market counterparties fail to fulfil their contractual obligations to
the company. Credit risk arises from cash and cash equivalents as well as from loans and receivables. Risk control assesses the credit quality of the
counterparty taking into account its financial position, past experience and other factors. Individual risk limits are set based on internal or external ratings
in accordance with limits set by the board. The utilisation of credit limits is regularly monitored. 
The Board of Directors has delegated responsibility for the management of credit risk to its bank Credit Committee. A separate bank Credit department is 
responsible for oversight of the bank’s credit risk, including:
Formulating credit policies in consultation with business units, covering collateral requirements, credit assessment, risk grading and reporting, 
documentary and legal procedures, and compliance with regulatory and statutory requirements.
Establishing the authorisation and structure for the approval and renewal of credit facilities. Authorisation limits are allocated to Head of Credit 
Committee. Larger facilities require approval by the the Board of Directors as appropriate.
Reviewing and assessing credit risk. The bank assesses all credit exposures in excess of designated limits, prior to facilities being committed to customers 
by the business unit concerned. Renewals and reviews of facilities are subject to the same review process.
Reviewing compliance of business units with agreed exposure limits, including those for selected industries, country risk and product types. Regular 
reports are provided to bank Credit  committee on the credit quality of local portfolios and appropriate corrective action is taken.
The estimation of credit exposure is complex and requires the use of models, as the value of a product varies with changes in market variables, expected 
cash flows and the passage of time. The assessment of credit risk of a portfolio of assets entails further estimations as to the likelihood of defaults occurring 
as well as of the associated loss ratios
The Company has developed models to support the quantification of the credit risk. These ratings  are in use for all key credit portfolios and form the basis 
for measuring default risks.  In measuring credit risk of loan and advances at a counterparty level, the Company considers three components: (i) the 
‘probability of default’ (PD) by the client or counterparty on its contractual obligations; (ii) current exposures to the counterparty and its likely future 
development, from which the Company derive the ‘exposure at default’ (EAD); and (iii) the likely recovery ratio on the defaulted obligations (the ‘loss 
given default’) (LGD). The models are reviewed regularly to monitor their robustness relative to actual performance and amended as necessary to optimise 
their effectiveness.
The Company assesses the probability of default of individual counterparties using internal rating tools tailored to the various categories of counterparty. 
The rating methods are subject to an annual validation and recalibration so that they reflect the latest projection in the light of all actually observed 
defaults.
EAD is is based on the amounts the Company expects to be owed at the time of default. For example, for a loan this is the face value. For a commitment, 
the Company includes any amount already drawn plus the further amount that may have been drawn by the time of default, should it occur.
Loss given default or loss severity represents the Company’s expectation of the extent of loss on a claim should default occur. It is expressed as percentage 
loss per unit of exposure. It typically varies by type of counterparty, type and seniority of claim and availability of collateral or other credit support.

CRITICAL JUDGEMENTS AND SIGNIFICANT ESTIMATES 
The Company makes estimates and assumptions concerning the future. The resulting accounting, estimates and assumptions that have a 
significant risk of causing a material adjustment to the carrying amounts of assets and liabilities within the next financial year are addressed below.
In accordance with IFRS 9 guidance, the company classifies some non-derivative financial assets with fixed or determinable payments and fixed 
maturity as held to maturity. This classification requires significant judgement. In making this judgement, the company evaluates its intention and 
ability to hold such investments to maturity. If the company were to fail to keep these investments to maturity other than for the specific 
circumstances – for example, selling an insignificant amount close to maturity – the company is required to reclassify the entire category as 
available for sale. Accordingly, the investments would be measured at fair value instead of amortised cost.
The Company reviews its loan portfolios to assess impairment regularly . In determining whether an impairment loss should be recorded in the 
statement of comprehensive income, the Bank makes judgments as to whether there is measurable decrease in the estimated future cash flows 
from a portfolio of loans before the decrease can be identified with an individual loan in that portfolio. Management uses estimates based on 
historical loss experience for assets with credit risk characteristics. The methodology and assumptions used for estimating both the amount and 
timing of future cash flows are reviewed regularly to reduce any differences between loss estimates and actual loss experience. For specific 
impairment the expected cash flows are discounted using the original effective interest rate when the loan was granted.

After losing his father while preparing to enter 200 level, Akeem faced serious financial challenges that threatened his education. Through a

ABAYOMI OYELEYE
Coming from a family of eight, Abayomi?s education was threatened when his father lost his job, leaving his mother as the family?s main
source of income. Paying his university fees became a major challenge, and he feared he might not complete his studies. LASAL Foundation
provided tuition support and a monthly stipend, allowing him to focus on his academics. He graduated with First Class honours and as the
Best Graduating Student in his department. Today, he is pursuing his PhD in Portugal.
GENESIS ADEWUYI
Before LASAL Foundation, Genesis struggled financially to pursue her dream of becoming a veterinarian. After spending an extra year

to balance work and her studies. LASAL Foundation provided the support she needed, covering her tuition, accommodation and monthly
expenses, while also investing in her personal development through mentorship. The Foundation’s support gave her the stability to focus on
her education and shaped her outlook on life. Today, Genesis has completed her Doctor of Veterinary Medicine degree, finished her NYSC,
and is practising as a freelance veterinarian, with a particular interest in avian medicine. She describes LASAL as a place that helped turn
